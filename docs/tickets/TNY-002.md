## Objective
Establish a maintainable technical baseline without prematurely selecting a provider or inventing unresolved product-policy values.

## Approved architectural direction
- Use a modular monolith: one web application, one primary relational database, and a small server-side API/function layer.
- Candidate frontend: React + TypeScript + Vite, as a client-rendered single-page application for the pilot.
- Database direction: PostgreSQL.
- Compare Supabase with at least one alternative before selecting the backend/hosting provider.
- Treat the NLe 500/month ceiling as a provisional limit for recurring platform costs. Verify real plan pricing, limits, required services, and likely workload before provider selection.
- Defer hosting and specific paid plans until the comparison is complete.
- Keep business responsibilities modular: price evidence; quality/governance; estimation; basket/history; affordability; savings/purchasing power; publication/audit; identity/access.
- Enforce authorization server-side and reinforce it with database controls where supported.
- Use automated domain, database/integration, authorization, end-to-end, and release-readiness tests.
- Preserve all unresolved product-policy decisions; architecture approval does not resolve them.

## Required provider evaluation
Compare at least Supabase with one alternative against:
- recurring platform cost within the provisional ceiling;
- PostgreSQL limits, storage, compute, connections, and likely workload;
- authentication, privileged TOTP 2FA, session controls, and recovery;
- server-side authorization and database policy support;
- backups, retention, restore process, and recovery cost;
- API/function limits, secrets handling, deployment, monitoring, and rollback;
- portability, operational burden, and vendor lock-in.

Record provider plans, pricing/limits, verification date, workload assumptions, exclusions, and risks. Do not treat a free tier or feature label as sufficient evidence. If no candidate satisfies required controls and budget, revise scope or evaluate another candidate rather than weakening security.
