## Objective

Establish a maintainable technical baseline without prematurely selecting a provider or inventing unresolved product-policy values.

## Approved architectural direction

- Use a modular monolith: one web application, one primary relational database, and a small server-side API/function layer.
- Candidate frontend: React + TypeScript + Vite, as a client-rendered single-page application for the pilot.
- Database direction: PostgreSQL.
- Prioritize Supabase for detailed cost and security evaluation; final provider selection remains pending.
- Treat NLe 500/month as the current ceiling for recurring platform costs. Any increase requires a separate explicit approval.
- Defer hosting and specific paid plans until the evidence and budget decision are complete.
- Keep business responsibilities modular: price evidence; quality/governance; estimation; basket/history; affordability; savings/purchasing power; publication/audit; identity/access.
- Enforce authorization server-side and reinforce it with database controls where supported.
- Use automated domain, database/integration, authorization, end-to-end, and release-readiness tests.
- Preserve all unresolved product-policy decisions; architecture approval does not resolve them.

## Supabase evaluation finding

The official Supabase pricing page and compute/billing documentation were re-checked on 2026-10-10. Pro is listed at USD 25/month and includes USD 10/month compute credits sufficient for one Micro instance, plus daily backups with seven-day retention. Using the provisional NLe 22.9194/USD planning rate, USD 25 is approximately NLe 573/month—about NLe 73 above the current ceiling before additional services, payment conversion costs, or usage overages. Seven-day PITR plus the required Small compute is estimated at approximately USD 130/month after assumed compute-credit application, before other services.

This is not a verified all-in local-currency quote. Cloudflare Pages Free is a candidate for static frontend hosting, and Resend Free is a candidate for low-volume authentication email; neither has been configured or validated for this project. Supabase's built-in SMTP is not production-ready and custom SMTP is needed for normal external auth email. A representative `.com` renewal is listed at USD 10.46/year (about USD 0.87/month before local conversion/fees); Backblaze B2 lists the first 10 GB free and USD 6/TB/month as an independent storage candidate. Neither service is selected, and operational export/restore costs remain unresolved. An unambiguous same-day exchange rate, actual payment fees, workload assumptions, and usage-dependent charges also remain unresolved. Supabase Free may be used for development or controlled experiments, but must not be described as the production recommendation without explicit acceptance of its limitations.

See [Supabase cost and security assessment v1](../architecture/supabase-cost-and-security-assessment-v1.md) for line items, security requirements, scenarios, failure conditions, and evidence still required.

## Required provider evaluation

The next evaluation step prioritizes Supabase and must verify:
- recurring platform cost, plan limits, included compute, storage, egress, and likely workload;
- static frontend hosting and production authentication email, including free-tier limits and domain verification;
- domain renewal price and actual payment conversion fees;
- authentication, privileged TOTP MFA enforcement, session controls, and recovery;
- server-side authorization, RLS, and database policy support;
- backups, retention, restore process, and recovery cost;
- API/function limits, secrets handling, deployment, monitoring, and rollback;
- portability, operational burden, and vendor lock-in;
- total recurring cost including required hosting, domain, email, backup/export, payment conversion, and overages.

Record source links, pricing/limits, verification date, workload assumptions, exclusions, and risks. If the minimum viable secure configuration exceeds NLe 500/month, document the gap and request a separate budget decision. Do not silently increase the ceiling or weaken required controls.
